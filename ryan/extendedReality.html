Extended Reality

What is Extended Reality?

Extended Reality (XR) is an umbrella term for technologies that combine the physical and digital worlds, including virtual reality, augmented reality, and mixed reality. XR allows users to interact with computer-generated environments and digital objects. It may even enhance real-world surroundings using digital technologies. Using devices such as headsets, cameras, sensors, and goggles, XR creates immersive and interactive experiences. It is applied in gaming, education, healthcare, training, and other industries. This technology can allow people to have new ways to interact with digital information and their surroundings, which can assist medical professionals using visual tools and improve learning through visual simulations.

The Pros and Cons

XR allows Canadian cultural institutions, including museums, film festivals, and art galleries, to create interactive experiences. This can expand audience engagement and provide new ways for people to experience and learn about art and culture.

XR supports economic development through gaming, software development, and new technology businesses. Montreal and Quebec have become important gaming and XR hubs, attracting investment and creating employment through innovation.

XR can create accessibility barriers for people with disabilities and individuals who cannot afford these devices for XR. Research identifies limitations in inclusive access and technical performance which potentially can restrict participation in education and other services.

XR artworks in Canadian cultural institutions may become difficult to maintain due to hardware and software becoming outdated rapidly. This creates financial and cultural challenges because institutions must preserve digital artworks while technology continues to change.
