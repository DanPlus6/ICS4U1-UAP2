Spatial Computing

Spatial Computing meaning

Agentic AI is a type of AI system that can accomplish a goal with a limited amount of supervision. It consists of AI agents and machine learning models that have the ability to mimic human decision-making to solve problems in real time. It's a system that uses multiple agents; each of the agents is requested to perform a specific subtask required to reach the goal, and it's coordinated through AI orchestration.

Pros and cons

  • P1:It allows users to blend real and virtual worlds seamlessly. Spatial computing can be useful for training, medicine, manufacturing, and architecture, as it allows people to be trained realistically without spending extra resources.


  • P2: Using spatial computing, people can work with 3D digital artifacts in virtual environments together. Scenes can be used to make a more immersive and interactive experience for people.


  • C1: Spatial computing requires very high computational power to render a complex 3D environment, which can be a problem, as it can be tough for the hardware to handle and needs an expensive setup or cloud computing to keep up.


  • C2: Another problem with spatial computing is that achieving low latency is hard to achieve; the lag between the real work movement and the digital space will break the immersive environment and experience.
